# Reranker Candidate-Depth and Latency Ablation

## Objective

This experiment evaluates the effect of hybrid candidate depth on final retrieval quality and latency after selecting `500_50` as the default chunking configuration.

The purpose is to determine how many candidates should be passed from hybrid retrieval to the cross-encoder reranker.

The comparison focuses on three candidate depths:

- `candidate_k = 10`
- `candidate_k = 20`
- `candidate_k = 40`

The final configuration is selected using four criteria, defined before interpreting the latency results:

1. Hybrid+Rerank Hit@5;
2. Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR);
3. stability of reranking, particularly whether existing Hybrid Top-5 hits are lost; and
4. latency and candidate-pool size.

The objective is therefore not to minimise latency alone or maximise a single ranking metric independently, but to identify the strongest observed quality–latency trade-off on the benchmark.

---

## Experimental setup

The experiment uses the previously selected `500_50` chunking configuration.

The benchmark contains 27 manually audited GRI 305-1 retrieval queries.

The following components are fixed across candidate-depth conditions:

- chunking configuration: `500_50`;
- benchmark queries and reviewed gold evidence;
- company-level metadata filtering;
- BM25 implementation;
- semantic embedding model: `text-embedding-3-small`;
- hybrid fusion method: Reciprocal Rank Fusion (RRF);
- cross-encoder reranker: `cross-encoder/mmarco-mMiniLMv2-L12-H384-v1`;
- final evaluation cutoff: Top 5.

Only the number of semantic and BM25 candidates admitted to RRF is varied.

For each query, semantic and BM25 rankings were first retrieved to the maximum evaluated depth of 40. The `candidate_k = 10`, `20`, and `40` conditions were then generated by truncating these same rankings before RRF.

This design keeps the underlying retrieval rankings fixed across candidate-depth conditions and isolates candidate depth from repeated embedding or retrieval calls.

---

## Candidate-pool interpretation

`candidate_k` specifies the number of candidates retrieved independently from each first-stage retrieval channel:

- semantic Top-k;
- BM25 Top-k.

RRF then merges these lists and removes duplicate chunk IDs.

Therefore, the fused candidate pool can contain more than `candidate_k` items and at most approximately `2 × candidate_k` items when the two retrieval channels have little overlap.

The observed median fused candidate-pool sizes were:

| candidate_k | Median fused pool | Mean fused pool |
|---|---:|---:|
| `10` | 16 | 16.4 |
| `20` | 32 | 32.6 |
| `40` | 62 | 61.9 |

These observed pool sizes are used when interpreting reranker cost.

---

## Retrieval-quality results

### Aggregate performance

| candidate_k | Hybrid Hit@5 | Hybrid MRR | Hybrid+Rerank Hit@5 | Hybrid+Rerank MRR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **`10`** | 0.926 (25/27) | 0.636 | **0.963 (26/27)** | **0.798** |
| `20` | 0.926 (25/27) | 0.649 | **0.963 (26/27)** | 0.779 |
| `40` | 0.926 (25/27) | **0.679** | 0.926 (25/27) | 0.772 |

The Hybrid Top-5 hit count is identical at all three candidate depths: 25/27.

This indicates that increasing candidate depth from 10 to 40 does not improve the number of relevant queries already appearing within the fused Hybrid Top 5.

However, the larger candidate pool changes the set of items available to the reranker and therefore changes the final ranking.

---

## Effect of reranking

The Hybrid and Hybrid+Rerank outputs were compared query by query.

| candidate_k | Recovered Hybrid misses | Lost Hybrid hits | Rank improved | Rank degraded |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **`10`** | 1 | **0** | **10** | 4 |
| `20` | 1 | **0** | 9 | 4 |
| `40` | 1 | 1 | 8 | 3 |

At both `candidate_k = 10` and `candidate_k = 20`, reranking:

- recovers one Hybrid Top-5 miss;
- loses no existing Hybrid Top-5 hits;
- raises final Hit@5 from 25/27 to 26/27.

The recovered query in both cases is:

`Siemens_305-1_slot_a`

At `candidate_k = 40`, reranking also recovers one Hybrid miss, but additionally removes one previously successful Hybrid Top-5 query:

`Schneider_305-1_slot_e`

As a result, the final Hit@5 remains 25/27 rather than increasing.

The experiment therefore does not support the assumption that exposing the reranker to more candidates monotonically improves final retrieval quality.

The observed result is narrower:

> On this benchmark, expanding the first-stage candidate depth from 20 to 40 did not improve Hybrid coverage and reduced final Hybrid+Rerank Hit@5 by one query.

---

## MRR behaviour

`candidate_k = 10` achieves the highest final Hybrid+Rerank MRR:

- `k=10`: 0.798
- `k=20`: 0.779
- `k=40`: 0.772

The difference between `k=10` and `k=20` occurs despite identical Hit@5 of 26/27.

This means that, among successful queries, relevant evidence is ranked somewhat earlier on average under `k=10`.

The benchmark is small, so these MRR differences should be treated descriptively rather than as evidence of statistically significant superiority.

Nevertheless, there is no observed ranking-quality advantage that would justify preferring `k=20` or `k=40` over `k=10` in the final reranked output.

## Latency methodology

Latency was measured separately for remote query embedding and local retrieval/reranking.

This separation is important because OpenAI embedding requests include network and service latency that is unrelated to local candidate depth.

### Query embedding

Each benchmark query was embedded once using:

`text-embedding-3-small`

The observed embedding latency was:

- median: 148.5 ms
- mean: 170.0 ms
- p95: 283.8 ms

These embeddings were cached and reused for local latency measurements.

### Local measurements

After one unrecorded warm-up pass, local retrieval and reranking were measured over three repeats of the 27-query benchmark.

The reported local measurements therefore contain:

- 81 observations per retrieval condition;
- no repeated OpenAI embedding calls inside the local timing loop.

This design isolates local retrieval and cross-encoder costs from network variability.

---

## Standalone retrieval latency

| Component | Median | Mean | p95 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| BM25 | 23.5 ms | 26.6 ms | 49.5 ms |
| Semantic vector lookup | 38.1 ms | 51.2 ms | 129.9 ms |

The semantic-local measurement excludes query embedding latency.

These values should therefore not be interpreted as complete end-to-end semantic-search latency.

---

## Candidate-depth latency

### Local pipeline latency

| candidate_k | Hybrid local median | Reranker incremental median | Hybrid+Rerank local median |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **`10`** | 28.1 ms | **853.6 ms** | **876.7 ms** |
| `20` | 30.2 ms | 1,770.6 ms | 1,801.3 ms |
| `40` | 32.9 ms | 3,190.8 ms | 3,226.2 ms |

### p95 local latency

| candidate_k | Hybrid local p95 | Reranker incremental p95 | Hybrid+Rerank local p95 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **`10`** | 42.2 ms | **1,312.2 ms** | **1,341.9 ms** |
| `20` | 45.3 ms | 2,383.1 ms | 2,468.5 ms |
| `40` | 100.6 ms | 4,326.1 ms | 4,367.0 ms |

The cost of increasing candidate depth is concentrated primarily in the cross-encoder stage rather than the hybrid retrieval stage.

Hybrid local median latency changes only modestly:

- 28.1 ms at `k=10`;
- 30.2 ms at `k=20`;
- 32.9 ms at `k=40`.

By contrast, median incremental reranker latency increases from:

- 853.6 ms at `k=10`;
- to 1,770.6 ms at `k=20`;
- to 3,190.8 ms at `k=40`.

This increase is consistent with the observed growth in fused candidate-pool size.

The experiment establishes an association between larger candidate pools and greater cross-encoder cost. It does not attempt to derive a general scaling law for reranker latency.

---

## Estimated end-to-end latency

For interpretability, an estimated median end-to-end latency was calculated as:

> median query-embedding latency + median local pipeline latency

The resulting estimates are:

| candidate_k | Estimated Hybrid E2E | Estimated Hybrid+Rerank E2E |
|---|---:|---:|
| **`10`** | 176.6 ms | **1,025.2 ms** |
| `20` | 178.7 ms | 1,949.8 ms |
| `40` | 181.4 ms | 3,374.7 ms |

These figures are estimates rather than direct end-to-end timing measurements.

They combine medians from separately measured components and therefore should not be interpreted as measured request-level latency distributions.

In particular, the estimated values are useful for comparing the approximate contribution of embedding and local processing, but p95 end-to-end latency cannot be derived by simply summing component p95 values.

A future production benchmark should measure complete API requests directly under representative concurrency and network conditions.

---

## Primary latency bottleneck

Under all three candidate-depth configurations, cross-encoder reranking dominates local retrieval latency.

At `candidate_k = 10`:

- hybrid local median: 28.1 ms;
- reranker incremental median: 853.6 ms.

The reranker therefore contributes substantially more local latency than first-stage hybrid retrieval.

The remote query-embedding median of 148.5 ms is also smaller than the median reranker cost under all evaluated candidate depths.

Within the current single-query benchmark environment, the cross-encoder is therefore the dominant measured latency component of the final retrieval stack.

This statement is specific to the tested hardware, models, implementation, and sequential query setting.

## Failure analysis

### Schneider 305-1 slot d

The remaining Hybrid+Rerank Top-5 miss at the selected chunking configuration was previously examined in detail under `candidate_k = 20`.

The relevant gold chunk was:

`2024_FR_SchneiderElectric_Sustainability_EN_p0142_c0002`

Its observed positions were:

- BM25 rank: 6
- semantic rank: outside Top 20
- RRF rank: 17
- cross-encoder rank: 24

The fused candidate pool contained 29 chunks.

This shows that the relevant evidence entered the first-stage candidate pool but was not promoted into the final Top 5.

It would be inaccurate to describe the failure as caused solely by reranking, because the relevant chunk was already outside the Hybrid Top 5 before the cross-encoder was applied.

The cross-encoder further demoted the candidate from RRF rank 17 to rank 24.

The appropriate attribution is therefore:

> Candidate generation recovered the relevant evidence, but fusion did not rank it within the Top 5, and cross-encoder reranking further reduced its relative position.

This gold label is retained unchanged.

---

### Schneider 305-1 slot e at candidate_k = 40

At `candidate_k = 40`, `Schneider_305-1_slot_e` is a Hybrid Top-5 hit but becomes a miss after reranking.

This is counted as a reranker-induced lost hit for the `k=40` condition.

The observation demonstrates that increasing candidate depth can change the cross-encoder ranking adversely for an already successful query.

The current experiment does not establish the mechanism responsible for the demotion.

Possible explanations such as increased competition from semantically similar candidates or additional contextual noise are hypotheses and are not required to justify the configuration decision.

The configuration decision depends only on the observed outcome:

- `k=40` does not improve Hybrid coverage;
- final Hit@5 is lower than at `k=10` and `k=20`;
- latency is substantially higher.

---

## Quality–latency trade-off

The final comparison is:

| candidate_k | Final Hit@5 | Final MRR | Lost hits | Median reranker latency | Estimated median E2E |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **`10`** | **0.963** | **0.798** | **0** | **853.6 ms** | **1,025.2 ms** |
| `20` | **0.963** | 0.779 | **0** | 1,770.6 ms | 1,949.8 ms |
| `40` | 0.926 | 0.772 | 1 | 3,190.8 ms | 3,374.7 ms |

`candidate_k = 10` and `candidate_k = 20` have identical final Hit@5.

However, `k=10` has:

- higher observed final MRR;
- no reranker-induced lost Top-5 hits;
- approximately half the median reranker latency of `k=20`;
- a substantially smaller fused candidate pool.

There is therefore no observed quality benefit in this benchmark that compensates for the additional cost of `k=20`.

`candidate_k = 40` is not competitive under the tested criteria because it is both slower and lower-performing on final Hit@5 than `k=10`.

---

## Configuration selection

**Selected candidate depth: `candidate_k = 10`**

The selection is based on the following jointly observed properties.

### 1. Best observed final ranking quality

`k=10` achieves:

- Hybrid+Rerank Hit@5: 26/27 = 0.963
- Hybrid+Rerank MRR: 0.798

Its Hit@5 matches `k=20`, while its MRR is higher.

### 2. Stable reranking behaviour

At `k=10`, reranking:

- recovers one Hybrid miss;
- loses no existing Hybrid Top-5 hit;
- improves the completion rank of ten queries.

### 3. Lower reranker cost

Median incremental reranker latency is:

- 853.6 ms at `k=10`;
- 1,770.6 ms at `k=20`;
- 3,190.8 ms at `k=40`.

### 4. No observed benefit from deeper candidate pools

Increasing candidate depth from 10 to 20 does not improve final Hit@5.

Increasing candidate depth to 40 reduces final Hit@5 from 26/27 to 25/27.

---

## Interpretation

The experiment supports selecting a relatively shallow reranker candidate pool for the current system.

On this 27-query GRI 305-1 benchmark, `candidate_k = 10` provides the strongest observed balance of:

- final retrieval coverage;
- ranking quality;
- reranking stability; and
- latency.

The evidence does **not** support a general claim that ten candidates are optimal for cross-encoder reranking in ESG retrieval systems.

The result is conditional on:

- the current benchmark;
- the `500_50` chunking configuration;
- the current BM25 and semantic retrievers;
- RRF fusion;
- `text-embedding-3-small`;
- `cross-encoder/mmarco-mMiniLMv2-L12-H384-v1`;
- the tested hardware and sequential execution environment.

---

## Limitations

### Benchmark size

The evaluation contains 27 manually audited queries.

This supports detailed deterministic error analysis but is not sufficient to establish statistically generalisable superiority of one candidate depth across ESG retrieval tasks.

### Candidate-depth range

Only three candidate depths were evaluated:

`10`, `20`, and `40`.

The experiment therefore does not prove that `10` is the mathematical optimum. It establishes that `10` is preferable among the tested configurations under the predefined evaluation criteria.

### Fixed retrieval architecture

The experiment holds the embedding model, lexical retriever, RRF implementation, and reranker fixed.

A different first-stage retriever or cross-encoder may exhibit a different candidate-depth trade-off.

### Latency environment

Latency was measured under sequential local execution on the development machine.

The results do not model:

- concurrent requests;
- batching;
- GPU inference;
- production server overhead;
- network contention;
- model-serving optimisation.

Absolute latency values should therefore be interpreted as development-environment measurements rather than production service-level guarantees.

### Estimated end-to-end latency

The reported end-to-end values are constructed from separately measured median embedding and local pipeline latencies.

They are intended for approximate component-level comparison and are not substitutes for direct request-level latency benchmarking.

---

## Decision

**Selected reranker candidate depth: `10`**

The subsequent verifier and end-to-end evaluations will use:

- chunking: `500_50`;
- hybrid retrieval: BM25 + semantic search with RRF;
- candidate depth: `10` per retrieval channel;
- final reranking: `cross-encoder/mmarco-mMiniLMv2-L12-H384-v1`;
- final retrieval cutoff: Top 5.

This configuration is selected because it preserves the highest observed Hit@5, achieves the highest observed final MRR among the tested candidate depths, introduces no reranker-induced Top-5 losses, and materially reduces reranking latency relative to deeper candidate pools.
